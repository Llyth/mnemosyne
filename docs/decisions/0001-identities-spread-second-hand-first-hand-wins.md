# Identities spread second-hand, first-hand wins

Mnemosyne shares identities peer to peer with the client's addon messages, with no server. Peers pass along what they know, so you can learn that Hugh Jass is Tony without ever meeting Hugh Jass: the network grows past the people you meet. Addon messages are not authenticated, so a second-hand identity can be false: it is shown apart from a first-hand one (heard from the character itself), and a first-hand identity always replaces it. We chose this over first-hand only, which is safe but reaches only your guild and groups, and over signed registrations, which need cryptography in pure Lua and a way to trust a key on first contact.

## Consequences

- Each character is registered on its own, by its player, on that character; nothing links an anonymous character to the player's others.
- Players can only describe themselves. Nobody can register another character, and there are no notes about other characters, not even private ones.
- Unregistering and staying anonymous have to spread second-hand too, or peers would bring a withdrawn identity back. Each change carries the character's revision, the game server's time of the change (or the previous revision plus one, if later, so it always moves forward), which survives a reinstall: among second-hand copies the later one wins, and the same revision with different nicknames is disputed and hidden until the character speaks. What peers say about one of your own characters is never adopted: it only pre-fills the registration prompt for you to confirm.
- A hostile peer can push false identities, so you keep at most 20,000 second-hand identities and accept at most 1,000 new ones per peer per day; first-hand identities never count toward either.

**Update, 2026-11-13.** The per-peer limit now counts every change: new identities, later revisions, removals and disputes alike. Known risk: revisions are trusted across hops, so a forged revision up to a day ahead beats every honest copy and spreads through syncs. The daily limit caps how many identities one peer can forge, but each forged one stays until the character changes its identity or is heard first-hand.

**Update, 2026-11-14.** A first-hand identity is never held back by a peer's limit when it reaches the next peer: a sync vouches for the first-hand changes of the last 23 hours it passes on (a line `F`; decision 0002), and the peer takes up to 5,000 vouched changes a day from one peer (`Memory.MAX_VOUCHED_PER_PEER`) besides its 1,000, while their revision is from the past day, never ahead of its clock (`Memory.VOUCHED_WINDOW`); one ahead counts toward the 1,000. A vouched copy is still second-hand, shown and ranked as any other, and passed on without vouching, so the claim goes one hop. Known risk, accepted: nothing proves the claim, so a hostile peer can now forge up to 6,000 identities a day instead of 1,000, each with a revision from the last day, which beats every honest copy older than it.

**Update, 2026-11-27.** This replaces "What peers say about one of your own characters is never adopted: it only pre-fills the registration prompt for you to confirm" for the memory, not for what is shown: what peers say about your own characters is now held and passed on as anyone's, second-hand, through syncs and guild news, under the same rules and daily limits. It is still never adopted: the tooltip, chat, the Identities tab, a lookup and the count of identities show your own registration (`Self`), never the copy, which is not shown as another player's character; the registration prompt still pre-fills from it only for a character not asked yet. A name another player's character has taken since is that character's, and its identity shows as any other's in all of them (decision 0003, `Self:owns`). The copy holds the GUID `Self` knows for your character, never one a peer passed on, and never goes stale on your character's own GUID: your character never speaks first-hand to its own account, so a stale copy would refuse every later revision for good and keep its bucket of the digest differing from every peer's. Before, a sync refused to learn or pass on what peers said about your characters once you had decided (registered, anonymous or unregistered), so each bucket of your digest lacked exactly their hashes, which every peer holding them had: a peer that compared its digest with yours named the characters your account lacked, from any of your characters, anonymous ones included, which linked an anonymous character to your others. The character played still never hears itself (no client hears its own messages), as for every peer. Known residual risks, not closed: the memory is the account's, shared by its characters, so two of your characters that sync with one peer within minutes send it the same digest, which another player's would match only if it held exactly the same identities; a peer that logs digests can link the two that way, more surely when one of them synced with it just before (that sync makes the peer's digest match the next one's). Buckets that stay differing because you forgot a character (`Memory:forget`) are the same from each of your characters, the version of Mnemosyne each whispers is the account's (it tells only between peers that run different releases, or a copy the packager did not build), and the game itself shows one character logging off as another logs on.

**Update, 2026-11-27.** This replaces "the memory is the account's, shared by its characters, so two of your characters that sync with one peer within minutes send it the same digest, which another player's would match only if it held exactly the same identities; a peer that logs digests can link the two that way, more surely when one of them synced with it just before (that sync makes the peer's digest match the next one's)" and "Buckets that stay differing because you forgot a character (`Memory:forget`) are the same from each of your characters" (the update above): each character now has its own memory, in a saved variable of its own (`MnemosyneCharacterDB`, `## SavedVariablesPerCharacter`): its identities and professions, the daily counts of each peer, the characters it forgot and the strangers it asked. Before, a hostile peer could plant something through one of your characters and read it back through another: a crafted whisper to one character gave it a first-hand change that another vouched for in its next sync within 23 hours, a dispute sent to one character hid that identity from every character's digest for good, and a peer that used up its daily limit on one was refused by all of them for a day. Your own characters' registrations, GUIDs and professions as read from the game (`Self`), the settings, the sync cooldowns (each character's own already, decision 0002) and the versions peers run stay the account's: no peer can plant them, and your own registrations still show on every character. At its first login after the update, each character starts from a copy of the account's memory without its disputes and daily counts; the account's memory (`MnemosyneDB.memory`, `.professions` and `.forgotten`) is kept as it was for 30 days, where 1.4.x still reads it after a downgrade, then dropped. A character played for the first time after that starts empty and learns on its own, at once from the guildmates and group members who answer its asks. Forgetting a character now holds for the character played only. Known residual risks, not closed: the copy is a past all your characters share, so what was planted in the account's memory before the update stays linkable until syncs replace it, the characters forgotten before the update stay forgotten on each, and a first-hand change of the last 23 hours is vouched by each character for up to 23 hours after its copy; the settings and the version of Mnemosyne are still the account's; and the game itself shows one character logging off as another logs on.

**Update, 2026-11-27.** This replaces "The copy holds the GUID `Self` knows for your character, never one a peer passed on, and never goes stale on your character's own GUID" (the first 2026-11-27 update): the memory holds what peers say about your characters as anyone's, the GUID a peer passed on included, kept by the same rules (the GUID held stays). Holding none, or `Self`'s, where every other player's memory holds the peer's, told a peer which names were yours: a hostile peer passed one of your characters on at a later revision with a made-up GUID and read back from your guild news or your next sync, without that GUID, that the name was one of yours, from any of your characters, anonymous ones included. Only the game showing one of your characters with the GUID `Self` knows for it (decision 0003) tells the name is yours: then a copy held of it is stale no more, whatever GUID it held, and a first-hand identity under its name, which only another player's character could have told and which no longer has the name, is dropped, so what peers say of yours comes in again. Each character's memory does the same when it loads, for each identity held with the GUID `Self` knows (`Memory.new`): a copy from the account's 1.4.x memory gone stale on your character's own GUID, or a first-hand identity of the name's former owner gone stale there, would otherwise stay so on every character that copied it, since two characters of one account are never online together, refusing every later revision and keeping a bucket of the digest differing from every peer's. A name `Self` knows no GUID for (a character not played since GUIDs were kept) is held as anyone's: a copy of it goes stale when the name shows up with another GUID than the one held, and one held stale stays so until that character is played and seen.

**Update, 2026-11-27.** A character created since the update now starts empty too, not from the copy (the second 2026-11-27 update): one the account's characters (`Self`, account-wide) did not know before its login, or knew under another GUID for its name (deleted or renamed since), is new (`Seed.isNew`). Before, every character whose saved variable had nothing in it got the copy, a brand-new one included, which then inherited the past your other characters shared, markers planted before the update among it, a link that matters most for a new anonymous character. The copy is made when the saved variables load, before the character's name is read (Mnemosyne reads it at `PLAYER_LOGIN`), so a new character has it emptied at login, before `Self` learns it and before any sync. A character of 1.4.x that never registered, was never kept anonymous and never had its professions read is not known to `Self` either, and starts empty as a new one does.
