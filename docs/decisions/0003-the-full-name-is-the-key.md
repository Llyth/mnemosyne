# The full name is the key

An identity is stored under the character's full name, `First Last`, exactly as the sender of an addon message gives it. On WoW Forever a full name is unique within the region and there are no realms, and the sender is set by the server, so a peer cannot speak first-hand for another character. We chose it over the GUID, which survives a rename, because no addon message carries the sender's GUID: a GUID inside the message would be a claim anyone could copy. A renamed character registers again as a new identity; the old one stays, with when it was last heard, until it is forgotten or the cap pushes it out.

Forever lets a player hide their surname in the world ("My Secondary Name"), so a tooltip can give only a first name, and first names repeat. For that case Mnemosyne keeps, for the session only, which GUID goes with which full name, learned from chat lines (they carry both) and from group members; the GUID is a lookup aid there, never a key.

A full name can come back: after a character is deleted or renamed, another player can take the name, and a stranger without Mnemosyne would show the old nickname forever, since nothing would ever correct it. So an identity also carries its character's GUID when Mnemosyne has seen it reliably (in a chat line or a group), and syncs pass it along. When the full name shows up with another GUID, the identity is stale: hidden everywhere until the new character speaks first-hand. A forged GUID can only hide an identity, never change it.

**Update, 2026-11-27.** This account's own characters keep their GUID too, read when each is played (`UnitGUID("player")`). A name one of them had shows that character's registration only while the GUID seen for the name (in the tooltip or chat line, else the one a first-hand identity holds) is its own or unknown; under another GUID the name belongs to another player's character, shown as any other. A character saved before then has no GUID until played again, and shows its registration as before.

**Update, 2026-11-27.** One question, `Self:owns(name, guid)`, says whether a name is one of this account's characters: by name while either GUID is unknown, not under a GUID other than the one `Self` holds for it. Recognition (the tooltip, chat, the Identities tab, a lookup and the count of identities), the memory and the professions all ask it. Before, only the tooltip and chat did: the Identities tab, a lookup and the count hid every identity held for a name one of your characters had, so another player's character that took it was missing there, and its professions were refused even first-hand. Now, once the GUID seen for the name (in a chat line or a group, else the one its first-hand identity holds) is not your character's, the name is that character's everywhere: its identity shows and counts as any other's, and the Identities tab lists it besides your character, which `Self` still lists as yours. A profession it tells first-hand with its GUID (`Professions:answer` sends your characters' own) is held as its own, replacing what was held as yours under that name, and your syncs leave the name to it. A peer's word on a GUID is never taken for one of your characters (`Self` knows theirs): the memory's copy of one (decision 0001) and its professions carry `Self`'s GUID and never go stale on it, and a first-hand identity under one of your names drops your GUID, since what speaks first-hand under that name is another player's character (no client hears itself or its account's others). Known limits: with no GUID at hand (a chat link's tooltip or a Communities line before the other character is seen, a first-hand profession from a version that sends no GUID, a character of yours not played since GUIDs were kept), the name still counts as yours; and the Professions tab shows your character's professions under that name, not the other's, while your character has professions read.

**Update, 2026-11-27.** For the memory, this replaces "A peer's word on a GUID is never taken for one of your characters (`Self` knows theirs): the memory's copy of one (decision 0001) [...] carry `Self`'s GUID and never go stale on it, and a first-hand identity under one of your names drops your GUID" (the update above): the memory's copy of one of your characters takes and keeps a peer's GUID as anyone's would (decision 0001), since holding none told a peer the name was yours. The memory never asks `Self:owns` with a GUID it holds: only the game showing the GUID `Self` knows for the name says it is your character's, and then the copy is stale no more and a first-hand identity under the name, another player's that no longer has it, is dropped. A first-hand identity under one of your names still drops the GUID `Self` knows for it; one a peer passed on, it keeps as anyone's.

**Update, 2026-11-27.** For the professions, this replaces "[the memory's copy of one (decision 0001)] and its professions carry `Self`'s GUID and never go stale on it" (the second update above): a copy of your character's professions that a peer passes on while it goes and comes as anyone's (decision 0008) takes and keeps the peer's GUID as anyone's would, since holding none told a peer, from an anonymous character too, that the name was yours. Your characters' professions as read from the game (`Professions:prepare`) still carry `Self`'s GUID, and still never go stale on it; but once the game shows the name with another GUID than the one `Self` knows (`Self:owns`), another player's character has taken it: what was held as yours goes stale under that GUID, your syncs stop passing it on and leave the name to that character, as 1.4.0 did on that chat line. Before, since the update above, your renamed or deleted character's professions went on being stamped and passed on under the name another player's character had taken, with your GUID.

**Update, 2026-11-27.** For a name another player's character has taken, this replaces "with no GUID at hand [...], the name still counts as yours" (the second update above), and how the professions told a taken name: `Self` now remembers, account-wide (`MnemosyneDB.characters`), that a name one of your characters had was seen taken (`Self:seeName`), when the game shows it with another GUID than the one `Self` holds for it, or when the character under that name tells its professions first-hand with another GUID. A GUID a peer passes on never counts. From then on `Self:owns` says the name is not yours by name too (a tooltip without a GUID, a peer's copy of that character's professions), for every character of the account, until one of your characters is played under the name again (`Self:see`). Before, only the professions held under the name told it, from the GUID held there, which could be one a peer passed on first (your old character's), and that went when they were dropped (the other character unseen on your roster and friends, at once when it never was on them, or 30 days after): your next sync took the name back and passed your old character's professions on again with your GUID, and peers' copies of the other character's professions were refused as yours.
