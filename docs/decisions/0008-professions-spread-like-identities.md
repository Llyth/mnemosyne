# Professions spread like identities

On Forever, Blizzard's guild professions view is off: `C_TradeSkillUI.IsGuildTradeSkillsEnabled()` returns false (checked in the game, 2026-11-24), so guildmates can't see each other's professions or recipes. Mnemosyne shares them instead: for each profession a registered character has (every one `GetProfessions()` returns, secondary ones included), its skill level and the recipes it has learned. Each of a character's professions has its own revision, is kept apart from its identity, and follows the same rules (decision 0001): first-hand wins, the later revision wins among second-hand copies, the same revision with different content is disputed and hidden, a second-hand profession shows with its source, and forgetting a character drops and refuses its professions. We chose this over a separate addon, which would rebuild the peers, the sync and the trust rules Mnemosyne already has, and over sharing on the guild channel only, which reaches guildmates online at the same time and nobody else.

## Consequences

- Only registered characters share. An anonymous character, or one not asked yet, shares nothing, and unregistering or choosing anonymous withdraws what was shared. Sharing is on by default, behind one account-wide "Share my professions" switch. Turning it off spreads a removal of each profession so peers forget, and dropping a profession spreads a removal of that one.
- Professions are kept only for characters on your guild roster and for your friends' characters, a character counting as a friend's for 30 days after it was last seen online as one. "Talk with everyone" (decision 0007) widens who you talk with, not whose professions you keep. A sync passes on only what the peer can keep: to a guildmate, the professions of your guild's roster and of your own characters; to a friend outside the guild, only your own characters'. A friend's professions reach you first-hand only. When a character falls outside what you keep (it leaves your guild, you leave it, or a friend's character goes 30 days unseen), its professions are dropped quietly on your side, without spreading a removal, since they haven't changed.
- A profession can go stale as an identity does: when its full name now belongs to another character, it is hidden until the new character shares first-hand.
- A character's professions show even when its identity doesn't (unknown, disputed or stale): listed by character name, with the nickname only when the identity shows. But the professions of a character whose identity you hold as a removal (anonymous or unregistered) are refused and dropped until its identity shows again, so no peer can link an anonymous character to a profession list.
- Nothing caps how many professions you keep besides that scope: a full guild of 1,000 characters with about 4 professions each is about 4,000, roughly 4 MB of saved variables, and a hostile peer can't push any outside the roster and friends.
- Professions spread through syncs only (decision 0002), never as news, with a digest of their own, and a sync passes them on after the identities, so a sync cut short has passed on the identities. A sync carries about 100 KB of them at most, first-hand ones changed in the past day first, so a newcomer fills in over several syncs. A peer accepts at most 2,000 professions a day from one peer, apart from the identity limits; your own characters' and first-hand ones don't count toward it. Professions don't expire by age: they stay until a removal comes or the character is forgotten. The new message kinds join protocol version 2, since a peer drops kinds it doesn't know.
- Your own professions are read from the game, never adopted from peers. Skill levels are read at login and when they change. The recipe list can only be read while that profession's window is open (`C_TradeSkillUI.GetAllRecipeIDs()` is empty otherwise, checked in the game), and holds the whole profession, learned or not. So the full list of recipe IDs is saved each time the player opens the window, and rechecked with `C_SpellBook.IsSpellKnown` at login and on `NEW_RECIPE_LEARNED`, which works with the window closed and tells learned from unlearned (checked in the game). Until a profession's window has been opened once, only its skill level is shared. Mnemosyne never opens a profession window by itself, and saves nothing from a window that isn't the player's own: a linked profession, the guild view or an NPC's crafting (`C_TradeSkillUI.IsTradeSkillLinked()`, `IsTradeSkillGuild()`, `IsNPCCrafting()`). Changes are saved as they are read, but a profession gets a new revision only when a sync starts, on either side, and only if it changed since its last one: only syncs carry professions, so peers hear as soon as with a revision at every change, and levelling a profession by hundreds of points changes the digest once per sync instead of at every point. The revision follows the identities' rule: the game server's time, or the previous revision plus one if that is later.
- A profession is named by its skill line ID (`GetProfessionInfo`), never by its name, which depends on the client's language.
- A profession carries recipe IDs only, about 90 of them, under 1 KB. Names and links are looked up when shown: `C_Spell.GetSpellName`, `C_TradeSkillUI.GetRecipeSchematic` and `C_TradeSkillUI.GetRecipeLink` work for a profession the character never learned (checked in the game). Forever's professions have a single tier (`GetChildProfessionInfos()` returns one).
- The "Show second-hand" switch covers professions too.
- Professions are shown in a window of Mnemosyne's own, opened from a button in the Escape menu and from `/mnemosyne professions`. The button sits in the menu's list, under Shop (or Options, and under EllesmereUI's buttons when they show), positioned from a `hooksecurefunc` on `GameMenuFrame`'s `Layout` that moves Blizzard's pooled buttons below it down and grows the menu, as EllesmereUI does; it is hidden in combat. It still never calls `AddButton` or hooks `InitButtons`: those would build the button inside Blizzard's pool and run its clicks tainted, while moving buttons only changes where they draw.

**Update, 2026-11-24.** Building the sync (#92) settled five details. A guild roster's character is kept like a friend's, for 30 days after it was last seen on the roster, which replaces "it leaves your guild, you leave it" among the ways a character falls outside what you keep (Consequences): the memory is shared by the account's characters, whose guilds may differ, and a roster read before the server sends it is empty, so dropping a character as soon as it is missing from the roster would drop another guild's professions or a whole guild's at login. Besides that scope, a profession whose skill line is not one of the game's (`Professions.SKILL_LINES`, first-hand or second-hand) is dropped, so a character holds at most the game's professions (12 on WoW Forever, which has no Jewelcrafting, Inscription or Archaeology): without that, a hostile peer could push 2,000 made-up skill lines a day onto one roster character, or any number onto itself, kept with no expiry. With **Pass on what I know** off, a sync still passes on your own characters' professions, which reach peers only through syncs, and none of the others. Your own characters' professions go only to a peer seen on the roster or as a friend's in the last 30 days, which keeps them; a stranger met through **Talk with everyone** would refuse them. Each side's digest of professions covers only what goes to that peer (the roster's and your own characters' to a guildmate, your own to a friend outside the guild), with **Pass on what I know** on or off, so two guildmates compare the same set; your characters that are not on the roster keep their buckets differing, and each such bucket is passed on again whole at each sync, both ways: every profession of every character in it that goes to the peer, the character's own (about 1 KB: two primary professions of about 90 recipes, some 330 bytes each, and the secondary ones) and those of the roster's characters that share its bucket (about 8 in a guild of 1,000). That is several KB for each of your characters off the roster at each sync, hourly with a guildmate since 1.2.0, within the 100 KB a sync passes on. As for identities, forgetting a character refuses and drops its second-hand professions only: a first-hand one still gets through. An anonymous character's professions are refused while its identity is held as a removal, even disputed or stale (`Memory:isWithdrawn`), so disputing the removal does not let them back in. One risk is accepted: a hostile peer can churn the memory's 20,000 second-hand identities until the removal is dropped as the oldest (about 4 days at the daily limits), after which that character's professions are no longer refused. A peer's recipe that the game places in another profession (`C_TradeSkillUI.GetTradeSkillLineForRecipe`, compared by `Professions.belongsTo`) is dropped, when heard and from the copies already held, since 1.1.x saved a window's recipes under the profession opened before; a recipe the game cannot place is kept, and a peer still on 1.1.x keeps a bucket differing and passes the same copy again at each sync, within the 100 KB a sync passes on.

**Update, 2026-11-24.** Two peers that synced in the 6 hours before they shared professions (one of them on 1.0.x, or both before updating to 1.1.0) would pass none on until the cooldown ended. A peer whose digest of professions this side never answered now skips the cooldown once, on either side, requesting a sync (`/mnemosyne sync` included) or answering the peer's digest. The saved `earlySyncs` table (`MnemosyneDB.earlySyncs`, peer -> `"heard"` or `"synced"`) ends that for good once the peer's digest of professions is answered or its early sync has started, so a 1.0.x peer, which never sends one, gets one early sync, not one at every trigger. Both sides apply the same rule, so after the update each pair lets one early sync through. The table is never pruned: one short entry per peer.

**Update, 2026-11-27.** This replaces "An anonymous character's professions are refused while its identity is held as a removal, even disputed or stale (`Memory:isWithdrawn`), so disputing the removal does not let them back in" (the first 2026-11-24 update): a disputed removal now refuses and drops only second-hand professions. A removal that disputes a nickname at the same revision now holds the identity as a removal whichever came first, so an anonymous character's second-hand professions stay refused and dropped (`Memory:isWithdrawn`). Any peer can forge such a removal with one guild news, so a disputed removal never refuses first-hand professions: what the character shares itself (first-hand) still gets through and stays, and an anonymous character shares only removals (`Self:professions`), so that links nothing. A removal that is not disputed still refuses and drops the character's first-hand professions too, which is how a peer that heard them from the character drops them once it goes anonymous or unregisters.

**Update, 2026-11-27.** For your anonymous and unregistered characters, this replaces "Your own characters' professions go only to a peer seen on the roster or as a friend's in the last 30 days" (the first 2026-11-24 update): their professions, all removals by then, now go, and count in the digest of professions, only while that character is the one played, so the peer hears them first-hand, from the character itself, which links nothing. Before, they went from whichever of your characters was played, to every guildmate and friend whose bucket differed; every peer holding the identity as a removal refuses them, so no other account ever held them, the bucket differed at every sync, and a peer that logged syncs could link the anonymous character to your account. While another of your characters is played, peers drop its professions through its identity's removal (`Professions:prune`). A peer that heard them first-hand and never took that removal (it held the character's registration first-hand, which a second-hand removal does not replace) keeps them, its bucket differing with your other characters at each sync, until the character itself syncs with it or goes 30 days unseen on its roster and friends.

**Update, 2026-11-27.** This replaces "a recipe the game cannot place is kept" (the first 2026-11-24 update): a peer's recipe the game cannot place (`C_TradeSkillUI.GetTradeSkillLineForRecipe` gives nothing) is now dropped too, when heard and from the copies already held. Keeping it let a hostile peer fill each copy with 600 made-up recipe IDs: 2,000 copies a day, about 1.2 million IDs and 33 MB of Lua memory, kept and passed on guild-wide. The lookup reads the game's own data, and Blizzard's UI treats its nil as "no recipe" (`NewRecipeLearnedAlertFrame_SetUp`, `ProfessionsUtil.OpenProfessionFrameToRecipe`), so a real recipe always gets a skill line. As a safeguard, every recipe is kept while the game has placed none this session (the lookup missing, or answering nothing), and a recipe the game places in a skill line Mnemosyne has no profession for is still kept: those are real recipes, a bounded set.

**Update, 2026-11-27.** This extends "dropping a profession spreads a removal of that one" (Consequences): a registered character now also spreads a removal of each of the game's skill lines (`Professions.SKILL_LINES`) it lacks, stamped once at its first sync after the update (`Self:stampProfessions`), so a profession a peer forged for it, one it never had, is dropped. Before, its share replaced only the skill lines it had, and nothing retracted another: a hostile peer could give a roster character a Fishing 300/300 with recipes, kept and passed on until the character went 30 days unseen. First-hand wins over any second-hand copy (decision 0001), so a peer that syncs with the character drops the forged copy and refuses it afterwards; one that hears the removal second-hand drops it too, unless the forger sends a later revision again, as for any second-hand copy. This needs no protocol change: a removal is the same message a dropped profession sends, which 1.1.0 and later (1.4.0 included) take for a skill line they hold or not. It costs up to 11 removals per registered character (about 25 bytes each, 50 with a GUID), each held by every peer that keeps the character's professions as a small entry, in the character's own bucket of the digest, so once passed on they change no digest. At first, those a peer passes on second-hand count toward the 2,000 professions a day it may bring, and can delay other professions it brings by a day. We chose this over making a character's first-hand professions in a sync a complete set that replaces every skill line held for it: a sync passes professions only in differing buckets and stops at 100 KB, so a receiver cannot tell a complete set without a protocol change.

**Update, 2026-11-27.** While the character played is not registered (anonymous, unregistered or not asked yet), this replaces, for your other characters, "to a guildmate, the professions of your guild's roster and of your own characters; to a friend outside the guild, only your own characters'" (Consequences), "With **Pass on what I know** off, a sync still passes on your own characters' professions" (the first 2026-11-24 update) and "Your own characters' professions are read from the game, never adopted from peers" with "a profession gets a new revision only when a sync starts, on either side" (Consequences): their professions now go and come as any other player's (`asAnyone` in `Professions`). They go only to a guildmate, only for a character on your guild roster and only with **Pass on what I know** on, in the digest and the answer alike, and are left out once you hold that character's identity as a removal, as a peer drops them then. Peers' copies of them are taken as anyone's: a profession you hold of them, read from the game, still wins, as for a peer that synced with that character itself. And they get no new revision in that session: what changed since their last one, or turning **Share my professions** off, reaches peers at the next sync of a registered character. Before, an anonymous character's syncs passed on its account's registered characters' professions to every guildmate and friend whose bucket differed, off the roster and with passing on off too, gave them revisions made in its own session, and refused peers' copies of them: only their own account did that, so a peer that logged syncs linked the anonymous character to them. The character played still shares its own professions first-hand, removals for an anonymous one, which links nothing. Known residual: a copy of your other characters' professions stamped at a sync start that reached no peer (the sync was cut first) is newer than any peer's, and goes out from the next session whichever character plays, a revision only your account holds.

**Update, 2026-11-27.** This replaces "The saved `earlySyncs` table (`MnemosyneDB.earlySyncs`, peer -> `"heard"` or `"synced"`)" (the second 2026-11-24 update): the early syncs are each character's own, as the cooldowns are (decision 0002), in `MnemosyneDB.characterEarlySyncs` (`MnemosyneDB.earlySyncs` is emptied, for a downgrade), `character -> peer -> "heard"` or `"synced"`, so a mark one character made never shows in another's syncs. Marks saved before, by peer only, are let go at load: each character may have one more early sync with a peer.

**Update, 2026-11-27.** This replaces "Until a profession's window has been opened once, only its skill level is shared" (Consequences): since #103 a character's learned recipes are also found with its windows closed. At login, and after a sync brings professions, each of its professions is checked against the recipes peers know for that skill line (`Professions:recipesToFind`): one counts as learned, and is shared from then on, when `C_SpellBook.IsSpellKnown` knows its spell and the game places it in that profession (`C_TradeSkillUI.GetTradeSkillLineForRecipe`), since lists 1.1.x read under the wrong skill line may have spread. A recipe no peer knows is still found only once the window is opened, so each character is asked once to open its professions' windows: in the registration prompt, or in the chat frame at login for a registered character none of whose professions has a saved list of recipes (`Self:recipeHint`).

**Update, 2026-11-27.** This replaces "the memory is shared by the account's characters, whose guilds may differ" (the first 2026-11-24 update): each character now has its own memory (decision 0001), its professions and the characters it saw on a roster or as a friend's included, so it keeps the professions of its own roster and friends, and its account's characters' go only to a peer that character saw. The 30 days stay, since a roster read before the server sends it is empty; at its first login after the update, each character starts from a copy of the account's, what was seen included.

**Update, 2026-11-27.** For your anonymous and unregistered characters, this extends "their professions now go and come as any other player's" (the update above, while the character played is not registered) to whichever character plays: while another of your characters plays, registered or not, an anonymous or unregistered character's professions go and come as anyone's (`asAnyone` in `Professions`). Peers' copies of them are taken under the same rules as any player's; what was held as that character's own (its removals, from its own session or from the seed) is dropped, as a peer drops them with its identity's removal; and they are never stamped: only your registered characters and the character played get a new revision when a sync starts, so an anonymous or unregistered character's removals reach peers from itself only. A registered character's own professions held while a character that is not registered plays are kept as a peer that synced with it keeps them, no longer as yours, so they are dropped as a peer drops them too. Before, while a registered character played, its syncs refused peers' copies of an anonymous or unregistered character's professions, which any other player takes: a hostile guildmate passing one on read from the registered character's digest of professions that the two share an account. An anonymous character never dropped what it held as a registered character's own (`Professions:prune` left it), so after a forged removal and a forged nickname its digest still held what every other player had dropped. And a registered character's sync stamped an anonymous one's removals with its own sync time, which the anonymous one later sent first-hand, telling when another character of its account was online.

**Update, 2026-11-27.** This replaces "at its first login after the update, each character starts from a copy of the account's, what was seen included" (the update on each character's own memory, above): a character created since the update, or one of 1.4.x that the account's characters never knew (never registered nor kept anonymous, its professions never read), starts with no professions held and nothing seen (decision 0001).

**Update, 2026-11-27.** This changes "after a sync brings professions" (the update on #103): the look for a character's recipes now runs once, 15 seconds after the last profession a sync brings, waits for the end of combat, and looks only at the professions peers brought since the last look (and at this account's other characters'); the look at login still reads every profession held. It ran half a second after each message that brought professions, about 25 times in a sync (they come about 4 seconds apart) and in combat too, and copied and sorted every profession held each time: 20 ms and 7 MB of garbage at 1,000 characters held. It now reads the professions in place, only those of the character's skill lines: under 2 ms after a sync of 400 professions, 4 ms at login, about 60 KB. A recipe learned during the session from a profession held before the look is found at the next login, or once its window is opened.
