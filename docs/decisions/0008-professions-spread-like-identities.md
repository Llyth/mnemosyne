# Professions spread like identities

On Forever, Blizzard's guild professions view is off: `C_TradeSkillUI.IsGuildTradeSkillsEnabled()` returns false (checked in the game, 2026-11-24), so guildmates can't see each other's professions or recipes. Mnemosyne shares them instead: for each profession a registered character has (every one `GetProfessions()` returns, secondary ones included), its skill level and the recipes it has learned. Each of a character's professions has its own revision, is kept apart from its identity, and follows the same rules (decision 0001): first-hand wins, the later revision wins among second-hand copies, the same revision with different content is disputed and hidden, a second-hand profession shows with its source, and forgetting a character drops and refuses its professions. We chose this over a separate addon, which would rebuild the peers, the sync and the trust rules Mnemosyne already has, and over sharing on the guild channel only, which reaches guildmates online at the same time and nobody else.

## Consequences

- Only registered characters share. An anonymous character, or one not asked yet, shares nothing, and unregistering or choosing anonymous withdraws what was shared. Sharing is on by default, behind one account-wide "Share my professions" switch. Turning it off spreads a removal of each profession so peers forget, and dropping a profession spreads a removal of that one.
- Professions are kept only for characters on your guild roster and for your friends' characters, a character counting as a friend's for 30 days after it was last seen online as one. "Talk with everyone" (decision 0007) widens who you talk with, not whose professions you keep. A sync passes on only what the peer can keep: to a guildmate, the professions of your guild's roster and of your own characters; to a friend outside the guild, only your own characters'. A friend's professions reach you first-hand only. When a character falls outside what you keep (it leaves your guild, you leave it, or a friend's character goes 30 days unseen), its professions are dropped quietly on your side, without spreading a removal, since they haven't changed.
- A profession can go stale as an identity does: when its full name now belongs to another character, it is hidden until the new character shares first-hand.
- A character's professions show even when its identity doesn't (unknown, disputed or stale): listed by character name, with the nickname only when the identity shows. But the professions of a character whose identity you hold as a removal (anonymous or unregistered) are refused and dropped until its identity shows again, so no peer can link an anonymous character to a profession list.
- Nothing caps how many professions you keep besides that scope: a full guild of 1,000 characters with about 4 professions each is about 4,000, roughly 4 MB of saved variables, and a hostile peer can't push any outside the roster and friends.
- Professions spread through syncs only (decision 0002), never as news, with a digest of their own, and a sync passes them on after the identities, so a sync cut short has passed on the identities. A sync carries about 100 KB of them at most, first-hand ones changed in the past day first, so a newcomer fills in over several syncs. A peer accepts at most 2,000 professions a day from one peer, apart from the identity limits; your own characters' and first-hand ones don't count toward it. Professions don't expire by age: they stay until a removal comes or the character is forgotten. The new message kinds join protocol version 2, since a peer drops kinds it doesn't know.
- Your own professions are read from the game, never adopted from peers. Skill levels are read at login and when they change. The recipe list can only be read while that profession's window is open (`C_TradeSkillUI.GetAllRecipeIDs()` is empty otherwise, checked in the game), and holds the whole profession, learned or not. So the full list of recipe IDs is saved each time the player opens the window, and rechecked with `C_SpellBook.IsSpellKnown` at login and on `NEW_RECIPE_LEARNED`, which works with the window closed and tells learned from unlearned (checked in the game). Until a profession's window has been opened once, only its skill level is shared. Mnemosyne never opens a profession window by itself, and saves nothing from a window that isn't the player's own: a linked profession, the guild view or an NPC's crafting (`C_TradeSkillUI.IsTradeSkillLinked()`, `IsTradeSkillGuild()`, `IsNPCCrafting()`). Changes are saved as they are read, but a profession gets a new revision only when a sync starts, on either side, and only if it changed since its last one: only syncs carry professions, so peers hear as soon as with a revision at every change, and levelling a profession by hundreds of points changes the digest once per sync instead of at every point. The revision follows the identities' rule: the game server's time, or the previous revision plus one if that is later.
- A profession is named by its skill line ID (`GetProfessionInfo`), never by its name, which depends on the client's language.
- A profession carries recipe IDs only, about 90 of them, under 1 KB. Names and links are looked up when shown: `C_Spell.GetSpellName`, `C_TradeSkillUI.GetRecipeSchematic` and `C_TradeSkillUI.GetRecipeLink` work for a profession the character never learned (checked in the game). Forever's professions have a single tier (`GetChildProfessionInfos()` returns one).
- The "Show second-hand" switch covers professions too.
- Professions are shown in a window of Mnemosyne's own, opened from a button in the Escape menu and from `/mnemosyne professions`. The button sits in the menu's list, under Shop (or Options, and under EllesmereUI's buttons when they show), positioned from a `hooksecurefunc` on `GameMenuFrame`'s `Layout` that moves Blizzard's pooled buttons below it down and grows the menu, as EllesmereUI does; it is hidden in combat. It still never calls `AddButton` or hooks `InitButtons`: those would build the button inside Blizzard's pool and run its clicks tainted, while moving buttons only changes where they draw.
