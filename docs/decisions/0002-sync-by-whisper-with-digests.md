# Sync by addon whisper, with digests

Two peers sync their memories with addon messages sent by whisper (`C_ChatInfo.SendAddonMessage` with `WHISPER`), invisible in chat and paced in the background. Each first sends a digest, its memory hashed in buckets of (character, revision), and then only the identities the other lacks or holds at a lower revision. We chose whispers over the guild or group channel because addon whispers outside instances are exempt from the per-prefix throttle (10 messages, then 1 per second), and a whole memory sent on a shared channel would reach, and cost, everyone on it. We chose digests over a database version number because two memories have no shared counter to compare. A sync happens at login with up to 3 peers heard recently in the guild, with each new group member who is a peer, and on `/mnemosyne sync`, at most once every 6 hours per peer. At most 2 syncs run at once and at most 10 wait, the most recently heard peers first; the rest are skipped and catch up through news or a later sync, so joining a raid full of peers does not back up the queue.

The guild and group channels carry only short messages. Announcements: a character says who it is and asks the others to. News: an identity a sync brought you new or at a higher revision is posted once to the guild, after a random delay of up to 30 seconds, and not at all if a guildmate posted it meanwhile, so the guild hears each change once and in seconds instead of at the next sync.

Everything goes out through one paced queue, about 1 KB per second in all, announcements and asks ahead of syncs, paused in combat and during lockdown (a boss encounter, a Mythic+ run, a PvP match, when the client blocks addon messages). A sync cut short starts again from the digest next time.

**Update, 2026-11-13.** A sync passes on at most 1,000 identities, what a peer keeps in a day from one peer (`Memory.MAX_NEW_PER_PEER`), starting at a point that moves with the clock so later syncs pass on the rest. A whole memory of 20,000 identities took about 830 KB and 14 minutes of the queue, which the peer mostly dropped, and nearly filled the sync queue, so a sync running beside it was cut; 1,000 take about 43 KB and 45 seconds.
