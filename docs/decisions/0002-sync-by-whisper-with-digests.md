# Sync by addon whisper, with digests

Two peers sync their memories with addon messages sent by whisper (`C_ChatInfo.SendAddonMessage` with `WHISPER`), invisible in chat and paced in the background. Each first sends a digest, its memory hashed in buckets of (character, revision), and then only the identities the other lacks or holds at a lower revision. We chose whispers over the guild or group channel because addon whispers outside instances are exempt from the per-prefix throttle (10 messages, then 1 per second), and a whole memory sent on a shared channel would reach, and cost, everyone on it. We chose digests over a database version number because two memories have no shared counter to compare. A sync happens at login with up to 3 peers heard recently in the guild, with each new group member who is a peer, and on `/mnemosyne sync`, at most once every 6 hours per peer. At most 2 syncs run at once and at most 10 wait, the most recently heard peers first; the rest are skipped and catch up through news or a later sync, so joining a raid full of peers does not back up the queue.

The guild and group channels carry only short messages. Announcements: a character says who it is and asks the others to. News: an identity a sync brought you new or at a higher revision is posted once to the guild, after a random delay of up to 30 seconds, and not at all if a guildmate posted it meanwhile, so the guild hears each change once and in seconds instead of at the next sync.

Everything goes out through one paced queue, about 1 KB per second in all, announcements and asks ahead of syncs, paused in combat and during lockdown (a boss encounter, a Mythic+ run, a PvP match, when the client blocks addon messages). A sync cut short starts again from the digest next time.

**Update, 2026-11-13.** A sync passes on at most 1,000 identities, what a peer keeps in a day from one peer (`Memory.MAX_CHANGES_PER_PEER`), starting at a point that moves with the clock so later syncs pass on the rest. A whole memory of 20,000 identities took about 830 KB and 14 minutes of the queue, which the peer mostly dropped, and nearly filled the sync queue, so a sync running beside it was cut; 1,000 take about 43 KB and 45 seconds.

**Update, 2026-11-13.** At most 30 news wait for their delay at once (`News.MAX_WAITING`); a sync that brings more, such as a first sync of a whole memory, posts 30 and leaves the rest to syncs, since the guild mostly holds them already. News heard is second-hand, with the guildmate as source, and is never posted again.

**Update, 2026-11-14.** Of what it passes on, a sync sends first the first-hand changes whose revision is within a day of the clock, either way (`Sync.FRESH`; one further ahead is a modified client's, which the peer refuses), the latest first, then the rest from the point that moves with the clock: a sync cut short (the peer went offline, a timeout) has still passed on what characters said of themselves lately, the copies that beat every second-hand one. Older first-hand identities take turns with second-hand ones: between two large memories nearly every digest bucket differs, so putting every first-hand identity first would fill the 1,000 with copies the peer already holds.

**Update, 2026-11-14.** Those first-hand changes of the last day go out vouched (decision 0001), up to 5,000 besides the 1,000 others; past 5,000 they take turns with the rest, not vouched. An older peer drops the vouching line and counts them toward its 1,000. The window for these changes, `Sync.FRESH`, is now the past 23 hours, never ahead of the clock (no real change is), an hour short of the peer's `Memory.VOUCHED_WINDOW`, so the last ones of a long sync still arrive inside it. Known limits: an answer at its largest is about 1,600 chunks, 5.5 minutes of the queue (26 in an instance, past the 20-minute cut, which then loses the unvouched tail), and the sync queue holds two or three of them; first-hand changes the peer already holds still go out in each bucket that differs, and a long run of them can reach the 2-minute cut. Both need thousands of first-hand changes made within a day.

**Update, 2026-11-14.** Protocol version 2 gives each chunked message an id, 1 to 99, the sender's next long message taking the next one: `2:C:<id>:<index>:<count>:<piece>`. Addon whispers come out of order on Forever, and version 1's reader held one message per sender, so two messages of the same chunk count whose chunks interleaved (the first's last chunk after the second's first) were spliced into one, and the line across the seam could join two identities into a wrong one, stored and passed on. The reader now keeps each message's chunks apart, at most 4 messages per sender (a fifth lets go the one heard from least lately) besides the minute's timeout. A version-1 peer drops every version-2 message as newer and tells its player once a session to update; a version-2 peer drops version 1 as older, so the two no longer hear each other until the older one updates.

**Update, 2026-11-14.** Addon whispers can arrive out of order, so a peer's end of its part can overtake its digest. A done before this side answered still ends the sync, frees its place and starts the cooldown (the peer may be refusing: cooling, or its waiting list full), but this side remembers it for 2 minutes (`Sync.TIMEOUT`) and answers the peer's digest once if it comes in that time. Before, the digest found the sync over and the peer got only a done, so both sides cooled for 6 hours with one memory not passed on. Identities that overtake the peer's digest, or a batch overtaken by the done, are still dropped: they come again in a later sync.

**Update, 2026-11-24.** The cooldown is now 1 hour with a guildmate (`Sync.COOLDOWN_GUILD`) and stays 6 hours with anyone else (`Sync.COOLDOWN`): a friend outside the guild, or a player met through **Talk with everyone**. Professions and nicknames then reach guildmates within an hour, while syncs outside the guild, which can be many with **Talk with everyone**, keep traffic low. A guildmate is a peer on this side's current guild roster, the one Client gives Professions (`Professions:seeLists`), which Sync reads; until the roster is read, or without Professions, every peer gets 6 hours. Each side applies its own roster, on both sides (requesting a sync and answering the peer's digest), and the one early sync for professions (the update above) is unchanged. `/mnemosyne status` and the window still list the syncs of the last 6 hours, guildmates included.

**Update, 2026-11-27.** Professions come after the identities (decision 0008), so a long run of identities or professions this side already holds reached the 2-minute cut and every profession after it was dropped, the cooldown already started. The cut still frees the sync's place: a batch this side already holds still does not keep the sync going, or a peer could hold a place by resending it. But in a sync this side answered, the peer's professions are still taken for 2 minutes after its last batch, as after its end of its part, and its batches (identities or professions) move that window only while the sync is inside its 20 minutes (`Sync.MAX_DURATION`), so a peer that keeps sending can push professions at most 2 minutes past the 20, within the daily limit of professions.

**Update, 2026-11-27.** A sync learns and passes on what the peer says about your own characters as anyone's (decision 0001): refusing them left your digest short of exactly your characters, bucket by bucket, which named them to a peer comparing digests.

**Update, 2026-11-27.** The cooldowns are now each character's own: "at most once every 6 hours per peer" (and the hour with a guildmate) counts the syncs of the character played only. The saved times move to `MnemosyneDB.characterSyncs`, keyed by character, then peer (`character -> peer -> time`); `MnemosyneDB.syncs` is emptied, so a downgrade to 1.4.0, which reads it as times by peer, still loads. Before, one account-wide table made a character refuse with a bare done a peer that another character of the account had synced with, which a peer that logged syncs could compare to link the two, an anonymous character included (decision 0001). Times saved before, keyed by peer only, are let go at load: each character may sync once more with a peer it synced with in the last hours, at most one extra sync. `/mnemosyne status` and the window list the character played's syncs, and `/mnemosyne sync reset` forgets every character's.

**Update, 2026-11-27.** This replaces "the one Client gives Professions (`Professions:seeLists`), which Sync reads; until the roster is read, or without Professions, every peer gets 6 hours" (the 2026-11-24 update on the guild cooldown): Client reads the friends and the guild roster once into `Scope`, which Sync and Professions both ask who is a guildmate (`Scope:isGuildmate`). Until the roster is read every peer still gets 6 hours; a Sync without Professions now gives a guildmate the hour too. Nothing changes for players: the addon's Sync always has Professions.

**Update, 2026-11-27.** An answer is made as the queue sends it, not whole in the frame the peer's digest comes. The queue asks a peer's answer for its next message only once that peer's queued chunks are sent, and the answer looks at about 1,000 names, or writes about 100 professions, between two looks at the queue (every quarter of a second); nothing is made in combat or lockdown, when the queue is paused. What goes out is the same: the same messages in the same order (the first-hand changes of the last day first, vouched, then the rest from the point that moves with the clock, then the professions), under the same limits, made from the memory as it is when each part is made. An answer stops once its sync is cut, and at most 3 peers' answers are made at once (`Outbox.MAX_PRODUCING`), as many as the sync queue held whole; a fourth is cut, as one past the queue's 4,000 chunks was. Answering a digest whole took about 80 ms and 13 MB in one frame at 21,000 identities and 2,000 professions held; the digest's frame now costs the start of the sync (about 7 ms: the professions' stamps and digest), and the answer at most about 6 ms in one look (the digest of professions it compares), most looks well under 1 ms.

**Update, 2026-11-27.** Whispers heard in combat or lockdown, a sync's mostly, now wait unread until it ends (at most 1,000, then each one more is dropped as a lost chunk), and are then read 10 at a time every quarter of a second, in the order they came. Nothing could be sent meanwhile, and reading them cost: a digest starts a sync (its stamps and digests, several milliseconds), and a batch of identities or professions takes 1 to 3 ms to hear. Messages on the guild and group channels are still read at once. Unread, they keep no sync going: one in a fight longer than 2 minutes is cut (`Sync.TIMEOUT`) even if the peer kept sending, and what it missed comes in a later sync.

**Update, 2026-11-27.** Client reads the friends and the guild roster into `Scope` once, at the first look (with the roster: one read empty in a guild had not come from the server yet, and is read again at the next look), then again at most every 30 seconds after they change, never in combat, the last read holding meanwhile. It read them again in the next message's frame after each change, every 10 to 20 seconds in a big guild: 1,000 roster entries, the friends, and the prune of professions (decision 0008), which now runs at most every 10 minutes as they are read (`Professions.PRUNE_EVERY`; characters are kept 30 days). A guildmate who joined, or a friend added, may wait up to 30 seconds to be in scope.

**Update, 2026-11-27.** No sync starts in combat or lockdown: one asked for then (a group member's announcement, `/mnemosyne sync`) waits in the waiting list, and the look every 5 seconds only cuts the syncs that went quiet or ran too long, starting the waiting ones once the pause ends. Starting one costs its professions' stamps and two digests, about 8 ms at 2,000 professions held, for messages that could not go out. That start now drops what is no longer kept of this account's characters only (`Professions:prune`), the rest at the lists' reads, every 10 minutes at most (the update above): 4 to 1.3 ms, 8 to 5 ms for the whole start.
